# F3: Navbar design patterns for fintech/loan websites

## Findings

### [1] Fintech/banking navbar login must be top-right and immediately visible, where users are conditioned to look for it
- quote: "The most critical calls-to-action (CTAs), like the online banking login, should be prominently displayed, ideally in the top right corner, where members are conditioned to look for it."
- url: https://www.pixelspoke.coop/ux-best-practices-for-credit-union-websites
- source_type: secondary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [2] Navigation should be organized around customer goals/tasks, not internal department structure
- quote: "Primary navigation should map to what users are trying to do, not to internal department structure. 'Personal,' 'Business,' 'Loans,' and 'Support' tend to work better than internal product line names customers don't recognize."
- url: https://advaitux.com/bank-website-ux-design/
- source_type: secondary
- published: August 3, 2026
- confidence: high

### [3] Top fintech sites use one primary CTA in the navbar with secondary CTAs subordinate in the hero
- quote: "Prioritizing one or two primary actions — such as 'Open an Account' or 'Check Rates' — improves both clarity and conversion." / "Primary calls to action should appear where users naturally look for them — near the top of relevant pages and repeated at logical decision points"
- url: https://advaitux.com/bank-website-ux-design/
- source_type: secondary
- published: August 3, 2026
- confidence: high

### [4] Leading fintech navbars use mega menus to organize broad product lines without overwhelming the top bar
- quote: "For institutions with broad product lines, mega menus can organize dozens of products without overwhelming the primary navigation bar — as long as they're grouped by customer goal (e.g., 'Buying a Home,' 'Saving for Retirement') rather than internal product codes."
- url: https://advaitux.com/bank-website-ux-design/
- source_type: secondary
- published: August 3, 2026
- confidence: high

### [5] Mobile banking apps have converged on horizontal bottom navigation with 4-5 items as the dominant pattern
- quote: "Banks have settled on a thumb-friendly consensus choice for mobile navigation: A horizontal main menu at the bottom of the screen. All but one firm (96%) in the Mobile Monitor coverage set offers this bottom navigation structure, with four or five elements (for an average of 4.57)."
- url: https://corporateinsight.com/mobile-navigation-report-ux-best-practices-for-financial-services-firms/
- source_type: primary
- published: August 20, 2025
- confidence: high

### [6] Vertical mobile menus have declined from 85% adoption in 2017 to just 1 firm in 2025 — hamburger-only is outdated for financial apps
- quote: "Vertical main menus, once popular, are on the outs. In 2017, 85% of the Mobile Monitor coverage set used vertical menus. By 2021, that figure dropped to 25%. Today just one holdout, PNC, still offers a 10-item vertical slideout menu."
- url: https://corporateinsight.com/mobile-navigation-report-ux-best-practices-for-financial-services-firms/
- source_type: primary
- published: August 20, 2025
- confidence: high

### [7] Betterment uses a sticky "Get started" CTA button on mobile that stays visible while scrolling
- quote: "A sticky 'Get started' CTA button that stays visible while scrolling"
- url: https://www.webstacks.com/blog/fintech-websites
- source_type: secondary
- published: August 21, 2026
- confidence: high

### [8] Stripe uses an organized dropdown mega menu to keep a broad product line easy to navigate
- quote: "An organized dropdown mega menu that keeps a broad product line easy to navigate"
- url: https://www.webstacks.com/blog/fintech-websites
- source_type: secondary
- published: August 21, 2026
- confidence: high

### [9] Navigation labels should use simple, conventional terms — creative/catchy names increase cognitive effort
- quote: "We often get requests from clients to be creative when it comes to navigation labels. We understand the impulse, but catchy names like 'Save & Spend' actually require more cognitive effort to understand and therefore detract from a seamless user experience."
- url: https://www.pixelspoke.coop/ux-best-practices-for-credit-union-websites
- source_type: secondary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [10] Fintech UX should apply progressive disclosure — show essentials first, let users dig deeper only if they choose
- quote: "Start with progressive disclosure. Show the essentials first, then reveal more details only when needed. Use smart defaults and contextual tooltips to guide users gently."
- url: https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/fintech-ux-best-practices
- source_type: secondary
- published: November 12, 2025
- confidence: high

### [11] Overflow/secondary navigation should be the final bottom nav item on mobile, or a hamburger in the upper area
- quote: "Firms can also rely on convention and user expectations: If an infrequently used capability is universal across industries, like notification settings, users will already have an idea of where to look for it in an app. ... Ideally, firms follow convention by housing overflow menus as the final horizontal menu item (79%)"
- url: https://corporateinsight.com/mobile-navigation-report-ux-best-practices-for-financial-services-firms/
- source_type: primary
- published: August 20, 2025
- confidence: high

### [12] Alchemy's navigation stands out for its layout, submenus, unique icons, and quick animations — iconography in nav supports quick scanning
- quote: "Its navigation bar stands out for its layout, submenus, unique icons and quick animations." / Hummingbird: "Precise iconography in the navigation menu that supports quick scanning"
- url: https://www.webstacks.com/blog/fintech-websites
- source_type: secondary
- published: August 21, 2026
- confidence: medium
